How it works

One transaction crosses both of Portaldot's VMs.

Off-chain
Reporters

Bond tPOT. Post BTC/USD from Coinbase, Kraken, Bitstamp, Gemini, Bitfinex.

→
Solidity
SextantCore

Rounds close at 2/3 of stake. Slashes, pays rewards, holds bonds.

⇄
ink! · Rust
SextantMath

Stake-weighted median, MAD, outlier mask.

→
Solidity
SextantFeed

Chainlink AggregatorV3Interface. Existing code reads it unchanged.

→
Consumer
MiniLend

Caps total debt at corruptionCost(). Rejects prices older than 15 min.

Rust does the maths, Solidity holds the money and speaks the interface every DeFi developer already knows.

What an attacker gets

Three outcomes, all enforced on-chain.

1 of 5 lies

Price holds

The median ignores the liar. The liar loses 20% of their bond. Honest reporters get paid.

2 of 5 collude

Feed freezes

Over 1/3 of the round disagrees. Nothing is published and no honest reporter is slashed.

4/9 of stake

That's the price

This much bonded capital can force a false price. The contract publishes the number as corruptionCost().

Why Portaldot

This design needs both VMs. Portaldot V3 is where they share one transaction.

  • ink! for the maths. Sorting and weighted medians over 16 reporters are cheap and memory-safe in Rust, at 10.4 KB of contract code.
  • Solidity for compatibility. Aave- or Compound-style code ports with zero oracle changes.
  • POT is the security budget. Every feed's safety is denominated in bonded POT, so feeds that matter create staking demand.
  • It unblocks the ecosystem. Lending, stablecoin, RWA and DEX builds here all need a price they can defend.

Business model

Apps pay per feed. Reporters earn for staking honestly.

Feed fees

Each feed is funded in POT by the apps that read it, and every honest report is paid from that pool.

Priced security

An app that needs more safety funds more stake. Its corruptionCost() rises, and its lending cap rises with it.

Premium feeds

FX, commodities and RWA attestations such as invoice or assay data, served through the same interface.
